# Editor decisions resolved at contract approval

This review was prepared while every contract remained `DRAFT_PENDING`. The
editor-in-chief approved all four recommended resolutions on 23 July 2026. The
changes were applied before the contracts and arc matrix moved to `APPROVED`.
`editor-approval.json` is authoritative for the approval state and exact file
digests.

## 01 — `first-farmers`

Current ending consequence:

> No text preserves the languages of the people who made it so.

This ends on a limit of the surviving record after the title has already stated
the historical result. It does not carry the consequence into the steppe
handoff, so it breaks the rule that a chapter ends on an irreversible result or
clear transfer.

**Approved resolution:** rewrite `ending.consequence` so it completes the
agricultural transformation and opens the steppe handoff:

> Fields, stores and inherited settlements had fixed Europe’s growing households
> to ground that the steppe expansion would seize and reorder.

## 03 — `bronze-europe`

Current thesis:

> Metal, ships and sacred power joined Europe to the older worlds of Egypt and the East.

The contract's causal spine, required emphases and governing judgement give
Europe's metalwork, seafaring, craftsmanship, sacred forms and heroic memory
their own agency. The thesis gives the governing sentence to Europe's connection
with Egypt and the East instead.

**Approved resolution:** recentre the thesis on Europe's appropriation and
mastery while retaining the older networks as a necessary cause:

> Drawing on older eastern networks, Europeans mastered metal and sea, forging
> distinct worlds of craft, sacred power, warrior courage and heroic memory.

## 06 — `empire-takes-cross`

The ending opens the next struggle on Constantinople's eastern frontier. The
handoff sends the launch Journey west to bishops, monasteries and kings in
`europe-reborn`. Those are two different next chapters. The later eastern Roman
continuation is explicitly deferred until the existing 24 chapters are complete.

**Approved resolution:** make Chapter 07 the immediate launch handoff and retain
the eastern struggle as a later world activation. Approved ending:

> **The Roman inheritance no longer depended on one western emperor.**
>
> Justinian’s empire preserved Roman law and Christian kingship from
> Constantinople. In the former western provinces, bishops, monasteries and kings
> now carried that inheritance through institutions able to survive without one
> imperial centre.

## 12 — `empire-many-liberties`

The ending reaches 1806 and then calls the frontiers chapter the “next trial.”
Chapter 13 begins with Visigothic resistance after 711 and moves through crusade,
frontier and siege material that predates 1806. The title “Liberty Has Learned to
Live Inside Order” is also an abstract judgement where the ending rule requires a
concrete irreversible result.

**Approved resolution:** use an explicit thematic return from 1806 into the
long frontier story. Approved fields:

> **The Empire ended; its jurisdictions remained.**
>
> When Francis II laid down the imperial crown in 1806, the Empire vanished as a
> polity. Its cities, territories, courts and archives preserved the habits of
> negotiated rule through which unequal powers had acted together for centuries.

Proposed handoff:

> To see what that capacity had defended, the Journey now follows Europe’s
> southern and eastern frontiers across the centuries before 1806.
