# Bronze Europe — navigation audit

- Surface: `/chapters/bronze-europe/`
- Viewport: `390 × 844`
- Flow captured: opening → act selector → second act
- Evidence:
  - `01-opening.png`
  - `02-act-selector.png`
  - `03-second-act.png`

## Overall verdict

The opening had the intended visual authority, but the experience ended as a short structural
demonstrator. The act tabs hid most of the historical sequence and made clicking, rather than
reading, the primary navigation model. The captured page contained 249 visible words across roughly
three mobile screens, producing about one to two minutes of use.

## Steps

1. **Opening — visually healthy, structurally incomplete**
   - The Great Sea map, ship scene, title hierarchy and bronze-on-green-black palette worked.
   - The CTA led to a separate selector rather than into a continuous historical sequence.

2. **Act selector — unhealthy**
   - Act II and III were hidden until selected.
   - The interface asked the reader to choose the next block instead of letting the narrative build
     through ordinary scrolling.
   - Tabs introduced keyboard and selected-state complexity without teaching history.

3. **Second act — incomplete**
   - Selecting act II changed a summary panel and movement list, but did not open a full narrative
     scene.
   - The page had no sustained route from metal and Crete through the Nordic Bronze Age, Uluburun,
     collapse and Homer.

## Required correction

- Keep every movement in one document flow.
- Let scrolling update place, date and map position automatically.
- Expand the public reading path to approximately 2,700–3,000 words.
- Keep interactions and evidence optional; neither may gate the next movement.
- Preserve the selected Great Sea opening and mobile-first visual grammar.
