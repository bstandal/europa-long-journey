# Greece and the Citizen — asset manifest

All narrative reconstructions were generated for this chapter with OpenAI ImageGen on 18 July 2026, then resized and encoded locally as WebP. They are evidence-led reconstructions rather than documentary photographs.

## Shared visual brief

Hard Mediterranean daylight; sun-warmed limestone; deep Aegean blue; terracotta; black-painted pottery; oxidized bronze; painted plaster, timber and rough stone. Ordinary bodies and civic work take priority over famous faces. Persia uses royal blue, patterned textile, administration and restrained gold, with individual people and no faceless mass.

Every prompt explicitly excluded readable text, modern objects, flags, white-marble postcard imagery, modern parliamentary chambers, fantasy armor, giant armies, blood and heroic combat poses.

## Narrative works

1. `01-after-palace`: a poet at a modest hall and a community gathering in a sanctuary court after palace administration.
2. `02-law-stands`: a damaged archaic law read aloud in public while the office holder stands apart from the stone.
3. `03-polis-crosses-sea`: settlers unload seed, household goods and protected fire at an inhabited shore shaped by negotiation.
4. `04-sparta-discipline`: divided Spartan civic authority, common mess and drill with unequal labor visible at the edges.
5. `05-athens-people`: a local registration joins inland, coastal and city routes into a new citizen geography.
6. `06-league-follows-sparta`: coalition command at Thermopylae and the nearby fleet, with Persian logistics treated as sophisticated.
7. `07-cities-survive`: ordinary allied rowers at Salamis resolve into the land coalition at Plataea.
8. `08-people-take-place`: an allotment token connects council, jury and open-air assembly.
9. `09-two-freedoms`: Athenian allied administration faces the looser congress around Sparta.
10. `10-war-no-field`: a displaced Attic household sees ravaging from the Long Walls as Sphacteria alters expectations.
11. `11-persia-gives-sea`: Persian silver, Cyrus, Lysander, ship repair and paid crews make sustained Spartan sea power visible.
12. `12-victory-trial`: return from Phyle opens into civic and federal continuity beyond the restored democracy.
13. `ending-rome-enters`: Roman officers encounter Greek magistrates, councillors and public records after the defeat of the Achaean League.

## Mobile crops and dedicated works

Dedicated vertical generations were used for the opening, Thermopylae, Salamis/Plataea, the Pnyx civic sequence, Persian-financed sea war and the Roman transition. All other scenes use the desktop reconstruction with the `mobileImagePosition` crop declared in chapter data.

## Map system

`attica-base-source.svg` and `greek-world-map-source.svg` in this directory are deliberately simplified editorial maps, not navigational products. Their derived WebP bases are deployed from `public/assets/chapters/greece-and-the-citizen/` below deterministic SVG overlays. Attica’s four states encode regions, demes, mixed tribes and the Council of 500. The nine war overlays encode only the decisive relationship described by each dated state; line weight and color distinguish Athenian maritime action from Spartan or allied pressure.
