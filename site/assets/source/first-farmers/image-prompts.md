# Image-generation prompts — The First Farmers

These are the production prompts used for the four replacement landscape assets.

## `harvest-had-to-last-v2.webp`

Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: full-bleed desktop and mobile chapter background, 3:2 landscape

Rebuild this scene as an archaeologically plausible Early Neolithic Thessalian farming landscape around 6500–6000 BC, while preserving the source image’s restrained cinematic realism, smoky dawn light, dark forest-and-ochre palette, and generous low-detail negative space on the left for ivory editorial text.

Scene/backdrop: a broad Thessalian basin and gently rising foothills, not a narrow river gorge and not sheer Iron Gates cliffs; cultivated emmer/einkorn plots near a small tell-like settlement; mixed open woodland at the margins.

Subject: a compact early farming settlement with mud-and-timber rectangular houses, bound sheaves, threshing/work area, baskets and a plausible protected grain store. A few distant people only; no heroic foreground figures.

Composition/framing: wide 1536×1024-style view; text-safe dark left 52%; historically informative settlement and storage details in the right half; natural atmospheric depth.

Lighting/mood: overcast late-harvest dawn, quiet, materially grounded, not fantasy.

Constraints: preserve the established EUROPA chapter visual language; plausible Neolithic construction and scale; no later raised medieval granary, no plank architecture, no roads, no metal, no writing, no decorative UI, no labels, no text, no watermark.

Avoid: dramatic canyon walls, alpine valley, medieval village density, regular modern fields, fantasy concept-art exaggeration.

## `more-mouths-more-land-v2.webp`

Use case: historical-scene
Asset type: full-bleed desktop and mobile chapter background, 3:2 landscape

Rebuild this scene as an archaeologically plausible Early Neolithic central European loess landscape around 5200–4800 BC, while preserving the source image’s restrained cinematic realism, smoky dawn light, dark forest-and-ochre palette, and generous low-detail negative space on the left for ivory editorial text.

Scene/backdrop: a broad loess terrace within substantial mixed deciduous woodland; several separated clearings connected loosely through the landscape, not a dense village and not a medieval field system.

Subject: a small number of widely spaced LBK timber longhouses at different stages of use, irregular cultivated plots close to the houses, woodland still dominant, a few distant people only. Show an expanding settlement footprint without modern regularity.

Composition/framing: wide 1536×1024-style view; text-safe dark left 48%; historically informative longhouses, fresh clearings, older regrowth and cultivation in the center and right; natural atmospheric depth.

Lighting/mood: quiet overcast dawn after rain, materially grounded, subtle signs of labor and woodland clearance, not fantasy.

Constraints: preserve the established EUROPA chapter visual language; plausible Neolithic longhouse construction and scale; no medieval village density, no uniform house rows, no gridded or fenced modern fields, no roads, no carts, no metal, no writing, no decorative UI, no labels, no text, no watermark.

Avoid: alpine valley, fortress settlement, thatched medieval cottages, picturesque fantasy concept-art exaggeration, regular parcels, vast deforestation.

## `one-house-one-clearing.webp`

Use case: historical-scene state variant
Asset type: full-bleed chapter landscape, 3:2 landscape

Create the earliest state of this exact Early Neolithic central European loess landscape around 5300 BC. Preserve the same camera position, river, horizon, atmospheric light, dark left text-safe area, palette and restrained cinematic realism. Replace the broad network of clearings with one modest irregular clearing at the forest edge and a single archaeologically plausible LBK timber longhouse with a very small cultivated plot. Continuous mature mixed woodland should dominate almost the entire right-hand plain. A few distant people only. The result should read immediately as the first household opening ground, before a village or settlement network formed.

Constraints: plausible Neolithic longhouse and scale; no medieval cottages, no gridded fields, no fences, no roads, no carts, no metal, no writing, no labels, no text, no watermark. Avoid fantasy and picturesque village density.

## `empty-houses-regrowth.webp`

Use case: historical-scene state variant
Asset type: full-bleed chapter landscape, 3:2 landscape

Create a later contraction state of this exact Early Neolithic central European loess landscape around 4500–4000 BC. Preserve the same camera position, river, horizon, atmospheric light, dark left text-safe area, palette and restrained cinematic realism. Show a previously used settlement zone after households have moved away: a few empty and weathered Neolithic longhouses, roof sections beginning to fail, unused irregular plots returning to grasses and scrub, young woodland regrowth around older clearings, no smoke and no visible crowd. Keep mature woodland substantial beyond the abandoned ground. The result should read as local contraction and movement, not catastrophe, battle or plague.

Constraints: archaeologically plausible Neolithic timber construction and scale; no corpses, fire, ruins of stone, medieval cottages, gridded fields, fences, roads, carts, metal, writing, labels, text or watermark. Avoid fantasy, apocalypse and dramatic destruction.
