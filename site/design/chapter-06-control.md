# Chapter 06 historical control

## Editorial claim

Christianity entered Roman law, office and imperial ceremony while Constantinople
became the centre of a renewed Christian Rome.

The chapter tells a Roman transformation, not the replacement of Rome by a
different civilization. It treats the eastern empire as the Roman state and
reserves later names such as “Byzantine” for historiographical clarification.

## Chronological boundary

- Opening: Constantine’s victory at the Milvian Bridge, 28 October 312.
- Closing: Justinian’s death, 14 November 565.
- Next pressure: the expensive wars with Sasanian Persia belong principally to
  the following chapter.

## Four acts

1. **The Sign and the Crown, 312–337** — victory, toleration, patronage and the
   foundation of Constantinople.
2. **Faith Becomes Imperial Business, 314–395** — councils, creed and the
   Christianization of imperial government.
3. **The Christian Capital, 395–518** — walls, institutions, Chalcedon and the
   survival of the Roman state after the western imperial office disappears.
4. **Roman Order in Stone and Law, 527–565** — Justinian’s law, Hagia Sophia,
   reconquest, plague and the cost of restored reach.

## Precision locks

- Constantine did not make Christianity the empire’s official religion in 312.
- The “Edict of Milan” was the agreement of Constantine and Licinius in 313;
  the text survives in Lactantius and Eusebius and included restitution.
- Nicaea in 325 did not end the fourth-century doctrinal conflict.
- Theodosius I’s Nicene settlement was a sequence: the edict *Cunctos populos*
  in 380, the council at Constantinople in 381 and subsequent enforcement.
- Constantinople was selected in 324 and dedicated in 330; the city continued
  to expand for generations.
- The Theodosian land wall was built under Theodosius II, conventionally dated
  to 413, with later repairs and additions.
- Chalcedon in 451 defined the council’s Christology and reorganized authority,
  while its reception divided major Christian regions.
- In 476 the western imperial office ended. The Roman Empire continued from
  Constantinople.
- Justinian’s legal project comprises the Codex of 529 and revised Codex of
  534, Digest and Institutes of 533, and later Novels.
- The Nika revolt occurred in 532; the rebuilt Hagia Sophia was dedicated in
  537 under the architects Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus.
- North Africa was recovered in 533–534 and the long Italian war ran from
  535–554. The first pandemic outbreak reached the empire in 541–542. Avoid
  invented population totals.

- 05 — Council: emperor, bishops, creed and reception.
- 08 — Capital: four stages of Constantinople’s physical and institutional reach.
- 13 — Sacred space: structure, light, rite and imperial presence in Hagia Sophia.
- Light three-stop traces: 03, 06, 09, 12 and 14.

## Visual system

Selected direction: **The Consecrated City**.

- Retain the project’s black iron, brick red, charcoal and smoky ivory.
- Add porphyry, liturgical indigo and sparse aged mosaic gold.
- Gold appears as reflected sacred light on uneven material, never as generic
  luxury trim.
- The Roman road rises into an avenue, arch, apse and dome.
- Imagery remains physically credible: stone dust, scaffolding, wax, smoke,
  worn cloth, rain on walls and reflected lamplight.
